Count what the rule allows

“Available” is the result of rules applied to a physical count, and two systems can apply different rules to the same stock without either making an arithmetic error. A promise is sound only when it draws on the stock the promising rule names.

You are the supply chain transformation lead at Sample Enterprise Goods. Customer service is about to promise the largest account’s order for its US stores: cases, due in five days, shipping on day 4. Eight status lines follow. Rule on each one against the promising rule, work out the promisable quantity, and say whether the promise holds the stock.

The promising rule
Count usable, sellable, uncommitted stock at US nodes, plus transfers with an observed pickup that are due before the ship date. Exclude the e-commerce reserve at the fulfillment center, Canadian nodes, held stock, and dispatches that are reported but not observed.
Usable, sellable, uncommitted
Usable removes held, damaged, or expired stock. Sellable applies product, location, ownership, and channel eligibility. Uncommitted removes stock assigned to other demand.
Observed
Shown by an independent event, such as a carrier pickup. The sending node’s own report of a dispatch is a report.
Reservation
An act that assigns specific units to an order. It succeeds only if the units are still free when it runs. Checking a figure, or calculating a promise from it, assigns nothing.
Promisable quantity
The cases the rule lets you count for this order.
