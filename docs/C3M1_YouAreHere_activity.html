Two views to fund

You lead supply chain transformation at Sample Enterprise Goods, a manufacturer and distributor of household and personal-care consumables. The executive committee has approved for AI and supply chain transformation, and the envelope is large enough to cover both proposals below.

Each proposal comes from a function that feels the cost of not seeing. The distribution and logistics director wants one view of inventory across the six distribution centers, the e-commerce fulfillment center, and its reserve. The procurement director wants to see inbound material before it arrives. Both argue that better visibility will improve decisions.

The two proposals on the table. The costs are the sponsors’ estimates.

    One word, two meanings

    The legacy warehouse system counts stock on quality hold as available, and the newer one does not. A single view over both adds two different quantities under one word, and a faster feed delivers the disagreement sooner.

    Warning needs somewhere to go

    Each sponsor was accurate about what they could see from their own function. Neither proposal says which action would differ if its signal changed.

    The capability this depends on

    Each option was a reasonable move inside one question: how much to see, and how fast. None began by asking which action would differ if the signal changed.

    That question has a test. A signal changes a decision only when it arrives before the point where the decision is committed, means what the decision needs it to mean, and finds at least one alternative still open. Tracing each signal to the decision it changes comes first, before any visibility investment is scoped, costed, or sequenced.
