Skill practice

Test each signal

A faster or wider signal helps only when it can change what someone does. For each signal, ask whether it reaches the decision before the decision is committed, whether it means what the decision needs, and whether any alternative is still open.

You are the supply chain transformation lead at Sample Enterprise Goods. Six signals are either proposed or already on a screen, and each one is aimed at a decision. For every pair, rate timing, meaning, and the open alternative, and then give a verdict.

Commitment point
The moment after which the decision is fixed. Latency is counted from the underlying event, not from the screen refresh.
Timing
Met when the signal reaches the decision before its commitment point. Fails when it arrives after. Met only with the proposed signal when it would be early enough but does not exist today.
Meaning
Met when the signal names the item, node, status, and quantity the decision needs, under a definition the decision can use. Fails when it does not.
Open alternative
Open when at least one feasible action could still differ if the signal took another value. None open when a policy, a passed window, a missing substitute, or a contract term fixes the action.
Verdict
Changes the decision when all three conditions hold. Only if a named gap closes when a condition fails and could be repaired. Changes no decision when no alternative is open.
