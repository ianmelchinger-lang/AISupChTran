Skill practice

Tag the evidence

An inbound record is worth what its evidence is worth. A supplier’s notice, a carrier’s pickup, a dock receipt, and a model’s estimate each support a different commitment, and an estimate means little without the time it was generated.

You are the supply chain transformation lead at Sample Enterprise Goods, reading the inbound status extract that buyers and planners use each week. For each of the six lines below, tag the evidence the line actually holds, then choose the strongest action that evidence supports.

Ordered
A purchase commitment exists. Nothing else about the shipment has been shown.
Reported shipped
The supplier’s own account of what it sent, such as a ship notice with item, quantity, and carrier.
Observed
An independent event shows the goods moving or arriving, such as a carrier pickup or a port arrival.
Received
The goods were counted at the receiving dock.
Predicted arrival
An estimate of when the shipment will land, recalculated as movement events arrive. It is usable only with the time it was generated.
The four actions
Commit fixes the schedule or the purchase timing to this shipment. Plan provisionally uses it in the plan and confirms at the next milestone. Treat as unconfirmed means asking the supplier or carrier first. Use the received quantity means planning on what arrived and returning the discrepancy to the buyer.
