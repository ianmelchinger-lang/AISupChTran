Judge each source

A shared view inherits what its sources have: their definitions, their delays, their errors. It can carry a decision only where the sources that decision draws on meet the conditions, and a source you cannot judge from the evidence is marked unknown, not met.

You are the supply chain transformation lead at Sample Enterprise Goods. The distribution and logistics director wants one inventory view across the distribution centers, the e-commerce fulfillment center, and its reserve. Before you sign, mark each source against four conditions, then rule on what the shared view can carry.

Common definitions
Each status means the same thing across sources, measured against the rule the decision uses.
Freshness and event sequencing
Events post soon enough, and in the right order, for the decision the view serves.
Named reconciliation owner
Someone is named to resolve differences between sources and correct them.
Records checked against counts
Physical counts confirm the records. The case sets the minimum cycle-count match for promising from a node without verification at .
Met, fails, unknown
Mark unknown when the case gives no evidence either way. An unknown is a question for someone to answer, and it is not the same as met.
The three rulings
Authoritative now means the view can decide this use as it stands. Usable for a named scope only means it can decide this use among the sources that meet the conditions. Not yet means a condition must close first, and you name which one.
