A decision record holds up when each step is written after the steps it draws on. Sort the cards, then give each step you keep a number.

The situation

You are the supply chain transformation lead at Sample Enterprise Goods. The senior vice president needs the demand-planning decision record: whether, where, and on what conditions to scale a forecasting change, and what any commitment would cost to undo. The pilot has been read by segment, and a vendor has proposed a three-year term. Ten cards follow. Some are steps on the path to a record you can defend. Others break a rule of the record.

In the order
A step the lead takes while building the record. Give each one a step number. Several orders can be sound, and the check flags only the ones that break a dependency.
Leave out
A card that breaks a rule of the record: it commits the company beyond what the evidence supports, or it writes a trigger outside the measures the record uses. It belongs in no step.
Dependency
A step that needs the output of another step before it can be written.
